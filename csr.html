A CSR-registered implementing agency in Nagpur for health, eye care, education, women's empowerment and the environment.

About the Foundation

Who you would be partnering with

Vishavabhartiya Foundation was incorporated on 12 March 2026 and carries forward the community health, eye-care and social service initiatives begun in 1998 by its founder and the earlier organisation, Vishvabhartiya Manav Kalyan Sewa Sanstha.

Governed by a three-member Board of Directors led by Dr. Dhananjay K. Yerne, Managing Director & CEO.

Geographic reach

Nagpur and Bhandara districts, Maharashtra

Urban Nagpur (slums, labour colonies, schools) and rural villages of Nagpur and Bhandara districts, where the founder's team has worked since 1998. Other locations can be considered with the partner.

Target beneficiaries

People who are hardest to reach

  • Low-income urban families and daily-wage labourers
  • Farmers and rural families
  • School children and adolescents
  • Women, the elderly and differently-abled people
  • Truck, taxi and bus drivers
Proposed project areas

Where a CSR partnership can start

Indicative project areas based on our existing work. Scope, budget and duration are finalised with each partner after a needs discussion — we do not publish fixed budgets.

Community eye-care & spectacles programme

  • Camps in slums, villages, schools and transport hubs
  • Refraction, spectacles, referral for treatment

School health & awareness

  • Vision screening of students
  • Hygiene, tobacco and environment sessions

Women's health camps

  • Screening and awareness for women
  • Linkage to government health schemes

Green neighbourhoods

  • Plantation with survival tracking
  • Plastic-reduction and cleanliness drives

Indicative budget

Prepared per project as a line-item budget after the needs discussion and shared with the proposal.

Project duration

Agreed with each partner in the MoU; can be a single campaign or a multi-month programme.

Outputs / KPIs

What we measure

Indicators we propose to track; targets are set per project.

  • People screened (by age group and gender)
  • Spectacles distributed
  • Patients referred and treatment completed
  • Camps held, by location
  • Students and women reached through awareness sessions
  • Saplings planted and surviving after 12 months
Implementation methodology

How a project runs

  1. 01Needs discussion

    Understand the partner's priorities, geography and budget.

  2. 02Proposal & budget

    Project proposal with activities, timeline, KPIs and a line-item budget.

  3. 03MoU & baseline

    Sign an MoU; record baseline data in the project area.

  4. 04Implementation

    Camps and sessions with registers, photographs and attendance records.

  5. 05Monitoring

    Periodic progress reports against agreed KPIs; field visits welcome.

  6. 06Closure & reporting

    Completion report, utilisation certificate and impact summary.

Monitoring & reporting

  • Camp registers and attendance sheets
  • Periodic progress reports against KPIs
  • Partner field visits welcome
  • Quality system certified to ISO 9001:2015

Documentation & impact reporting

  • Photographs with date and location (with consent)
  • Utilisation certificate and completion report
  • Impact summary for the partner's CSR report
  • Receipts as applicable
